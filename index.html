The Glow Up for Eventbrite

A pitch by Rea Shah for Bending Spoons' newest product

In this pitch 01 · Eventbrite in numbers 02 · The acquisition 03 · What isn't working 04 · My four ideas 05 · Where I'd start 06 · To sum up

01 Eventbrite in numbers

If you've been to a comedy night, supper club or run club in the last few years, there's a good chance you booked it on Eventbrite. In 2024 it sold 83 million paid tickets to 4.7 million events, across almost 180 countries, with 89 million people using it every month. (Eventbrite Q1 2025 results)

02 The acquisition

Bending Spoons agreed to buy Eventbrite for about $500M in cash, or $4.50 a share, and the deal closed on 10 March 2026. Bending Spoons' website says it's still working on its first improvements, so I had a go at making a list.

03 What I think isn't working

Tap a problem to open it.

1 Organisers are selling fewer tickets

Revenue dropped 14% in Q2 2025 and 8% in Q3 compared with the year before. On an earnings call, management said organisers were selling fewer tickets each than they'd expected. If organisers sell less, they have less reason to stay.

2 It's hard to reach a real person

Reviewers say they can only get through to an AI chatbot, usually when they need help most. A consumer journalist had the same problem when he tried to contact them.

3 People lose trust when events are cancelled

Some buyers say organisers cancelled and never refunded them, and Eventbrite said it couldn't do anything. One reviewer suggested Eventbrite should hold the money until the event actually happens. I think they're onto something.

4 The app is useful, but it isn't fun

Eventbrite relaunched its app in 2025 and it does look cleaner. But it still feels like scrolling through listings. When I use Partiful or DICE, I can see who's going and it feels like part of the night out. I don't get that on Eventbrite.

5 Reselling a ticket means leaving the app

Eventbrite works with partners like Tixel and Twickets for resale, but only if the organiser signs up, and there's no simple way to transfer a ticket to a friend's account.

04 My four ideas

Make the app more fun and a lot smarter

The look: bigger type, bolder colours and better event photos. You'd be able to see which friends are going before you buy, and share your ticket to your Instagram Story or group chat.

The features:

  • Plan my night. Type something like "fun and cheap in Hackney on Friday" and an AI assistant puts together a plan for you.
  • A "For you" page based on the events you've booked before.
  • A live map of what's happening near you tonight.
  • Wallet tickets that update by themselves if the time or venue changes.
Why I think it's worth it

After the 2025 relaunch, paid tickets bought in the app went up 11% and monthly app users went up 13%. People are already moving to the app. And Bending Spoons uses AI across everything it does, so the assistant is a natural fit.

How I'd test it

Release one feature at a time to a group of users, and check whether more of them buy a first ticket and come back to book again. I'd also keep an eye on existing users, to make sure the changes don't put them off buying.

Let people swap tickets inside the app

If you can't go, you list your ticket at the price you paid. Whoever buys it gets a new ticket, so there's no risk of a fake or a screenshot. The UK government is planning to make reselling above face value illegal and make platforms responsible for enforcing it, so building this now means Eventbrite is ready before the law arrives. It should also mean fewer refund requests and fewer empty seats, and Eventbrite still earns a fee on each swap.

How I'd test it

Start with a group of UK organisers and track how many listed tickets sell and whether refund requests drop. I'd also check it doesn't eat into normal ticket sales.

Bring a friend

Going out is social: people go to things their friends are going to.

So straight after you buy a ticket, the app would ask who's coming with you and give you a link to send them. Since organisers are selling fewer tickets each, this could help where it matters most.

How I'd test it

Show the prompt to half of buyers and not the other half, then compare tickets sold per event. I'd also watch unsubscribe rates in case people find it pushy.

Build trust into the platform

  • Verified badges and quicker payouts for organisers with a good track record
  • New organisers get paid after their event happens, not before
  • Buyers get refunded automatically if an event is cancelled
  • An easy way to talk to a real person when something goes wrong
How I'd test it

Add a verified badge to some new events and not others, and see if more people complete their purchase. The risk is that new organisers might not like waiting to be paid, so I'd track how many of them stop using Eventbrite.

05 Where I'd start

  1. It's quick to build and easy to test.

  2. The trust changes, plus a UK pilot for ticket swaps.

  3. One feature at a time, starting with the "For you" page.

Eventbrite Ads are already doing well. They grew 38% in Q3 2025 and organisers who use them sell 4x more tickets. A more personal app would give those ads more people to reach.

+38%Eventbrite Ads growth, Q3 2025

06 To sum up

Eventbrite has millions of events and users. I think the next step is making the app something people enjoy opening, not just somewhere they go to buy a ticket.
